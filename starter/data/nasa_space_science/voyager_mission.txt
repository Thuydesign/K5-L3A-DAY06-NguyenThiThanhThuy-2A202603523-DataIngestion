What’s on the Golden Record

Voyager 1 and its twin Voyager 2 are the only spacecraft ever to operate outside the heliosphere, the protective bubble of particles and magnetic fields generated by the Sun. Voyager 1 reached the interstellar boundary in 2012, while Voyager 2 (traveling slower and in a different direction than its twin) reached it in 2018. Now Voyager 1 is on the brink of another unprecedented milestone — reaching a distance one light-day away from Earth.

‘Shine Bright for Voyager’s Light-Day’

Help celebrate Voyager 1's epic milestone — NASA invites everyone in the world to create a symbolic wave of golden light encircling the planet after sunset on Tuesday, Nov. 17, by flashing a message to the spacecraft with phones, flashlights, and even entire buildings. See how to join in!

What Is a Light-Day?

The milestone Voyager 1 will reach on Wednesday, Nov. 18, is about distance, not time. And it's very, very far — more than 16 billion miles, during a journey that's taken 49+ years, from our cosmic neighborhood out to interstellar space. Learn all about it.
